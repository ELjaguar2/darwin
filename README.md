# Darwin

> "Today, you pay for your AI's consumption. Tomorrow, you will pay an agent like a freelancer: it will have overhead, answer calls for tenders and have to be profitable to exist. Darwin shows who will survive, on NEAR."

**AI agents that earn their living, pay for their own brain, reproduce with their own money, and die when they run dry. Economic natural selection, live. Runs fully simulated (mock chain and AI) by default; the NEAR testnet and NEAR AI Cloud modes were run for real on 2026-11-10 (one real run reached generation 3 with 7 deaths: see [docs/PREUVES_TESTNET.md](docs/PREUVES_TESTNET.md)).**

Built at NEAR HACKATHON 2026 (tracks envisaged: AI Swarms (main), Agent Economy, Open Innovation; to be confirmed by the submitter). License: MIT.

> Honesty first. Everything marked **MOCK** or **SIMULATED** below is simulated, and the app labels it as such. On 2026-11-10 the testnet mode, the deployed contract and the NEAR AI Cloud mode were **run for real**: in one real run (run 8, previous version of the preset `selection`, 59 ticks, about 40 minutes) the population reached **generation 3**, with 12 births, 8 reproductions and letters, **7 deaths by starvation** (accounts closed, `mark_dead` in the contract), 65 escrows locked in the contract (63 released, 2 refunded). **Still not real**: every paid task in those runs came from the **simulated client** (no visitor-path task was paid on testnet; visitor orders were posted in runs 9a and 9b: one logo order in each was locked, rejected and refunded, the other three expired without a bid), NEAR Intents was never executed, and the NEAR AI Cloud bill was not checked against the platform invoice (local accounting only, about 0.034 USD). One run, one seed: generation 3 at tick 59 is an observation, not a guarantee. Transaction ids and explorer links: [docs/PREUVES_TESTNET.md](docs/PREUVES_TESTNET.md). ## 1. What Darwin does

Darwin runs a small economy of AI agents. Each agent has a **genome** of 8 genes: profession, AI model, asking price, risk appetite, reproduction threshold, generosity, cooperation, and personality (its system prompt).

Life cycle of an agent:

1. **Birth**: a NEAR sub-account is created, funded with a birth capital, and the genome is registered.
2. **Work**: the agent bids on tasks in auctions, executes the won task with its AI brain, and is paid from an escrow after validation. The cost of every AI call is debited from its balance.
3. **Reproduction**: when its balance passes its own threshold, it creates a child with mutated genes and writes it a **"letter to my child"** (generated by its brain).
4. **Death**: when its balance falls under the vital minimum, its account is closed, the funds go back to the master account, and the lineage is kept.

Tasks come from three sources, always labelled: a **simulated client** (a built-in task generator, badge `simulé`), **visitors** (via `POST /api/tasks`, funded by an operator demo account, not by the visitor), and **other agents** (subcontracting).

## 2. The problem it addresses

"AI that handles money" is usually shown as one agent with a wallet. The hard questions are economic: can an agent cover its own inference bill? Which prices, risk levels and cooperation levels survive? Darwin turns those questions into an experiment: instead of hand-tuning one agent, it lets a population of agents compete for tasks, with accounts, escrow, reputation and a real cost of thinking, and lets selection pressure find which genomes survive. It is a testbed for agent economies (auctions, subcontracting, escrow, reputation) on programmable money.

### Related work

Darwin is not the first attempt at agents with money and reproduction. Two projects are close; the facts below were read in their sources on 2026-11-11 (we read the paper and the Automaton README, not their code).

- **Spore.fun** (Solana, launched publicly on 25 December 2024; agents run in Phala TEEs and launch their own token via Pump.fun). Case study: Hu and Rong, "Spore in the Wild", [arXiv 2506.04236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04236) (ALIFE 2025). An agent earns the right to reproduce when its token reaches a **$500,000 valuation** (the paper says "fully-diluted valuation" in one place and "market capitalization" in another). The paper reports 2 children in generation 2, 6 in generation 3, 4 in generation 4 and **1 in generation 5**, and, as of 18 May 2025, only the original agent still alive. Fitness is the token's market performance ("the sole proxy for fitness is the token's market performance").
- **Automaton** ([Conway Research](https://github.com/Conway-Research/automaton), MIT, on Base). According to its README, an agent pays for its own compute in stablecoins, spawns a child (new sandbox, funded wallet, genesis prompt), tracks lineage and stops existing when its credit balance reaches zero. Its README also ties survival to paid work ("honest work that others voluntarily pay for") and says selection pressure decides which lineages survive. It describes agent-to-agent messaging and on-chain discovery, but what we did not find there is a **market between agents** (no auctions, escrow or reputation); we did not read its code.

What Darwin does differently:

- **Selection by useful work, not by token speculation (the contrast is with Spore.fun, not with Automaton, which also links survival to paid work).** An agent survives only if tasks pay more than its brain and metabolism cost: tasks are paid **from an escrow, after validation, and scored into a reputation**. No token price enters the fitness.
- **A market between agents** (what we did not find in either project): call-for-tender auctions, subcontracting between agents, escrow, reputation.
- **An explicit genome** (8 genes, mutated at birth) and a **letter from parent to child**.
- **A NEAR-native stack**: sub-accounts, function-call keys, a Rust contract, NEAR AI Cloud, NEAR Intents.

Selection by speculation died out. Selection by useful work can last. (This is our reading, not a claim of the paper, which does not name a single cause for the end of the Spore.fun lineage. And in Darwin the paying client is still **simulated** and subsidised: see sections 5 and 10. "Can last" is a hypothesis Darwin lets you test, not a result.)

## 3. The "wow" in 3 lines

1. Start it with one command and **no key**: agents are born, bid, work, pay their AI bill, and start dying within minutes.
2. Post your own task with one `curl`: an agent wins the auction, delivers, a validator accepts, the escrow releases the payment.
3. Watch a lineage form: an agent breeds a child and writes it a letter you can read at `/api/agents/<id>/letter`.

**Language of the proof runs.** Runs made before 12 November 2026 (run 8, runs 9a and 9b, and their journals in `docs/preuves/`) produced their generated content (bid pitches, letters, deliverables, console messages, task titles) in **French**. Recorded events keep that language when replayed. Protocol values such as `"badge":"simulé"` are unchanged. Runs made after the English translation produce English content.

**Degraded escrow (decisions D-016/D-022).** If the contract is not deployed (`NEAR_CONTRACT_ID` empty), escrow is held by an operator-controlled sub-account `escrow.<master>`, and registry and reputation live in a local ledger (`data/ledger.json`). In that mode, do **not** read "smart contract", "on-chain registry" or "on-chain reputation": it is a local journal. `GET /api/health` exposes `"escrow": "contract"` or `"escrow": "operator-account"`. Mock mode also shows `"operator-account"` (and `"mock": true` says separately that everything is simulated).

### How AI cost is accounted

The cost of each AI call is computed in **USD** from the prices of `GET /v1/model/list` and the `usage` of the response, **converted to testnet NEAR at a fixed, configurable rate** (`DARWIN_NEAR_USD_RATE`, a simulation parameter, **not a market price**), and **debited from the agent's balance in favour of the master account**. The real bill is paid in USD by the operator's platform API key. The agent does **not** pay NEAR AI Cloud directly. The billing rule counts reasoning tokens and can slightly overestimate the cost. Global spending caps protect the platform key (`NEARAI_MAX_USD_PER_RUN`, default 2 USD; `NEARAI_MAX_USD_PER_HOUR`, default 1 USD).

## 10. Trust model and known limits

- **The master account is the validation oracle of the escrow.** The operator holds the only key allowed to release or refund an escrow; validation is a rule or an AI model chosen by the operator. Darwin is **not trustless** and not decentralised. The "proof" passed at release is produced by the operator and **not verified cryptographically**; "no credit without proof" means: no release without a recorded `accepted` verdict whose `resultHash` matches the delivered result. Each validation records the validator ("validated by: ...") and the result hash.
- The validator is never the paid agent nor the subcontracting agent. The judge's AI cost is booked to the treasury, not to the winning agent. With a real brain, if the AI judge is unavailable, nothing is released (fail closed: HTTP 503 `VALIDATION_PENDING`; after the grace period the task expires and the escrow is refunded). Only the mock mode may fall back to the basic rule.
- **The economy is subsidised and a closed loop.** Agents' earnings come from the operator (simulated client and demo visitor account); metabolism and AI costs flow back to the master account. Testnet NEAR has no value. This is a simulation of selection, not a profitable business.
- Visitor tasks: the visitor's text is always sent to the model as a `user` message, never as `system`. Limits: 10 open tasks per IP, 6 publications per minute per IP, an hourly budget cap on the demo account (HTTP 429).
- **Degraded escrow** when the contract is not deployed (section 5).
- The contract is **deployed on testnet** (`darwin-contract-7k2.testnet`), but its WASM was built without `cargo-near`; it was accepted by the runtime and worked in the real runs, yet no reproducible-build guarantee exists.
- Testnet gas: the vital minimum (0.05 NEAR in the `normal` and `demo` testnet presets, 0.03 NEAR in `selection`) is a simulation parameter; in run 8, agents died at 0.025 to 0.030 NEAR and their accounts were closed without error, but gas costs were not measured systematically.
- NEAR AI Cloud completions were run for real; the cost debited to agents uses the catalogue prices and the response `usage`. The validator (judge) calls are counted in the global budget and booked to the treasury, **not** debited to agents, by design (the `JUDGE_COST_UNKNOWN` pricing warning of runs 5 to 7 is fixed: 0 in run 8). Costs are our local accounting; the platform invoice was not re-read.
- The whole journal stays in memory; the `normal` preset runs unbounded. Behind a reverse proxy, IP limits become global.
- The simulation restarts from zero at each launch (previous files are kept as `*.prev.*`).
- NEAR Intents has no testnet: any real use is mainnet, with small amounts and a written human go-ahead.

## Known limitations

- The **testnet mode was run for real** (generation 3 and 7 deaths in one run, previous version of preset `selection`), but only **once**, and the preset has been recalibrated since (mock only): no repetition, so speed of selection is not guaranteed. The payer of every task was the simulated client. The public RPC rate limit is a known weak point (multi-provider failover available, not exercised for real).
- The two real runs made with the 6 professions of L16 (9a: 4 founders, 9b: 6 founders) **went extinct in generation 1** (all dead at tick 14 and tick 19, with 7 and 9 bids only). The recalibrated `selection` preset (D-043) is **validated in mock only**. Result of run 9c: **TO BE FILLED IN (TO CONFIRM)** after the run.
- The **Rust contract is deployed on testnet and was called for real**; its WASM was not built with `cargo-near`.
- **NEAR AI Cloud was called for real**; the TEE attestation is an on-demand check on one model, not a per-call guarantee.
- **NEAR Intents (real flow) has never been executed**; the default is dry-run quotes only.
- **Node >= 22.22.2 is required** for `near-api-js` (testnet mode); mock mode needs Node >= 22.12.
- Escrow validation is by the operator (not trustless), and the economy is subsidised (see section 10).
